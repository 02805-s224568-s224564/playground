# Self-review before publishing

The course's rubric for peer feedback is two lines: **what works**, and **one
concrete thing to try**. Apply it to our own draft first, then run the
checklist. Compare the draft with `notebooks/briefs/week<N>.md` so the review
knows which tools the week asked for.

## Output format

### What works

Two to four bullets, each specific: quote the sentence, name the figure, or
cite the number that earns the praise.

### One concrete thing to try

One suggestion. Doable in under an hour. Phrased as an action a group member
can start on right away, for example "Plot the same thing for out-degree; the
asymmetry is the story." If several come to mind, pick the one that would
change a reader's takeaway the most.

### Checklist

One PASS or FAIL per line, with a word on why for each FAIL:

- Uses this week's tools or ideas (from the brief).
- Four parts in order after the lede: what we asked, what we did, one figure or table, what surprised us.
- At most two figures or tables, and the prose reads every one of them. Figures have alt text and a caption, and are embedded with the include, not with markdown image syntax.
- "What surprised us" has substance; it is not the finding restated.
- Every number in the post appears in the notebook. Open the notebook and check.
- `notebook:` in the front matter points at an existing file; `week` matches the filename and the brief.
- The lede paragraph stands alone as an excerpt.
- No `<!-- go-nuts:` markers left. No `layout:` in the front matter.
- 300 to 600 words; no headings beyond the four.
- Figure files are named `week<N>-<slug>.png` or `.html`.

## Rules for the reviewer

- Quote the draft. Do not paraphrase what it should have said.
- One concrete thing means one.
- Do not edit anything. `copyedit` edits; `review` does not.
- Findings, numbers and interpretations are the authors'. If one looks wrong, say so as a question in the checklist, never as a correction.
